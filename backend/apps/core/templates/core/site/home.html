How Spekooh works

From a photo of a past paper to a verified marking guide

Informal WhatsApp groups and photocopies are unreliable and unverified. Spekooh turns that into a structured, incentivized pipeline, open to every level from Primary school to Concours des Grandes Écoles.

1

Submit a paper

Anyone, student or not, uploads a photo, PDF or scan and tags subject, level, exam board and year.

2

A verified instructor reviews it

The paper is routed to a verified instructor on our partner platform, who accepts and writes the marking guide.

3

The guide comes back merged

Our review team checks it, finalises the objective/MCQ answer key in-house, and publishes the combined guide.

4

Everyone can read and practice

Papers stay open to browse for free; marking guides unlock per-guide or with Spekooh Pro. Contributors earn credits.

Why we built Spekooh

The team behind Spekooh

Students across Cameroon, at every level and in both the Anglophone and Francophone systems, have long relied on scattered, informal sources for past exam papers: photocopies passed around, screenshots in WhatsApp groups, papers with no marking guide at all. None of it is verified, and none of it is centralized.

We built Spekooh to fix that. One place to find past papers for every level, from Primary school through Secondary (GCE, BEPC, Probatoire, Baccalauréat), University, Tertiary/HND and the Concours des Grandes Écoles, with each paper routed to a verified instructor who writes a real marking guide: not a guess, not a leaked "correction" of unknown origin.

Spekooh is bilingual (English/French) from day one, because the exam systems it serves are too. Contributors, students or anyone with a paper to share, earn credits for what they submit; instructors are paid to mark. It's a structured, incentivized pipeline instead of an informal favour economy.

Spekooh is one product from a small team that also builds a few other things. We like solving problems that make daily life in Cameroon a little more organised.
